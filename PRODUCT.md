# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People evaluating the studio's work: prospective clients, collaborators and design/3D peers who land on the page
from a portfolio link, usually on a laptop or large desktop screen, sometimes on a phone. They scroll the whole
story once, linger on the 3D moments, and judge craft: motion, framing, typography, how the details hold up.

## Product Purpose

Raven is a concept site for a medical delivery drone serving remote mountain villages and cold-climate communities.
It exists to showcase scroll-driven 3D storytelling and interface craft through a credible, specific subject.
Success: a visitor finishes the story impressed by the execution and reaches out ("Discuss a use case").
It is a concept, not a product launch: no invented specs, ratings or confirmations.

## Brand Personality

Rugged, capable, trustworthy. Field equipment, not a gadget: built for cold, altitude and bad roads, described plainly.
Confidence comes from precision and restraint (exact framing, honest copy, measured motion), not from hype.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS: gradient cards, icon-feature grids, hero-metric rows, pastel blobs.
- Charity pity imagery: guilt-driven humanitarian tropes, suffering as decoration.
- Toy/consumer drone marketing: playful DJI-style gadget launch, glossy lifestyle shots, "epic" adjectives.

## Design Principles

1. Show the machine working. The 3D drone and terrain carry the story; UI frames them and never competes.
2. Honest over impressive. Labels are illustrative; never fake telemetry, numbers or outcomes.
3. Equipment-grade precision. Every overlay, tick and label is aligned, legible and purposeful, like an instrument.
4. Restraint is the flourish. One strong move per moment; quiet everywhere else.
5. Craft holds at every size. Phone, laptop and wide screens each get a deliberate composition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Text over the live 3D scene must stay readable (contrast against the busiest frame, not the average).
Full keyboard access for nav, menu and contact dialog; visible focus. `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps the story
readable without continuous motion (rotors still, no idle sway).
